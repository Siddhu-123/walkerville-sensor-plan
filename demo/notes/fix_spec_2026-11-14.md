# Fix spec for the Sim 4 deck audit (14 Nov 2026)

The audit is `demo/notes/reviews/2026-11-14_sim4_deck_audit.md`. This spec turns it into work. Every number must come from `demo/notes/sim4_source/sim4.js`, `demo/notes/sim4_source/regions.js` or `demo/sim4-regions.js`, which is generated by `demo/tools/build_sim4_regions.py` from those files.

Writing rules apply to all copy:
- Plain, careful engineering-student English.
- No em dashes.
- No "What we did:" / "The result:" scaffolding.
- No meta-commentary.
- Never write "SA Water said/wants".
- Never state a cost ratio such as "25x" or "5-6x".
- Call the university "Adelaide University".
- Label assumed values as assumed.

## Content that must be stated honestly (audit section 2)

Each item names where it goes: a card or an info card.

- **S1-A:** every headline uses the assumed 10 mm rise rule.
  - At 25 mm, 5 sensors see 36% worst / 45% average.
  - Sensor 1 reacts +12.4 mm at 14 homes in R3.
  - Normal levels at Stonyfell vary 20-75 mm (Do et al. 2023, `levelsensor` chip).
  - Whether 10 mm of growth can be told apart from day-to-day variation is untested. Put this in the sensors card or its info card, and show the 25 mm line on the ladder chart.
- **S1-B:** the five come from a greedy, nested ladder.
  - An exact solver gets 88% worst with a different five, and at least 96 five-sensor sets tie on the design wet day.
  - Present the sensors as zones with stand-ins: MH4450531 has 4, MH4450922 has 3, MH4452994 has 23, MH4448345 and MH4428268 have 0.
  - Goes in the sensors or holdup info card.
- **S1-C:** on a design wet day, 2041 growth newly surcharges no manhole. The 11 overloads all come from wetter cases (50 of 315 wet runs show a new surcharge, 1 a spill). Wet weather matters more than growth. This is the frame for the problem slide and the welcome subtitle.
- **S1-D:** triage uses the design wet case only, while the five-sensor plan uses all 7 wet cases.
  - Only R5 keeps room in all 7 wet cases.
  - R1 (Watch) runs out in 3 of 7 cases, as R3 does. R8 is already over the limit in 4 of 7.
  - The regions card says this plainly, and the region info card gives the per-case bands.
- **S2-E:** "16 of 16" is 16 runs, and "11 of 11" is 11 region-weather pairs. One run moves a case's score by 5-7 points, so 94% and 92% are the same within noise. Goes in an info card.
- **S2-F:**
  - In-sample 94% falls to 77% on unseen weather and 76% on an unseen region.
  - Sim 4 calls the unseen-region figure "a little generous" because R9 overlaps other regions.
  - With the age-weighted design wet case held out, even 8 sensors see 0% of it.
  - Goes in the holdup card or its info card.
- **S2-G:** wet-weather level records (not normal-day records) are what would decide between one sensor and five. Reaching 100% of all 7 wet cases takes 10 sensors (90% takes 6). Drop "save up to 4 sensors (about $100k)".
- **S2-H:** floats only count if they sit 25 mm above normal (assumed), and the prices are assumed. Floats on their own stall at 35% average / 0% worst. In a mix, $75k buys 2 level sensors + 6 floats ($56k) and reaches 62% worst, against 44% for 3 level sensors. State no price ratio.
- **S2-I:** growth was tested at 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5 and 3 times each region's 2041 figure. The "homes to limit" ranges go past 2041 for that reason.
- **S2-J:** write "adapted from Kim et al. (2022)". Sim 4 cuts at pipe ends instead of manholes, includes loops, and lets a manhole sit in two regions. Kim et al. chose sites for disease surveillance, not capacity.
- **Sensor stability:** sensors 1 (MH4450531) and 3 (MH4450922) appear in the October deck's five, the 6 November Sim 3.5 five and Sim 4's five. Put this in the holdup card, replacing "4 of the 5 sensors stay the same".
- **485 homes:** this is the regions.js anchor. Cite Sim 4 for it. The underlying planning projection source isn't in the repo, so the assumed info card lists it as a source still needed.
- **Welcome page:** replace "Project #2669" everywhere with "Capstone PG-S2-22". Remove `project_no`.
